AI Events Finder · Switzerland

Find AI events worth your evening.

A curated list of AI events across Switzerland. An AI assistant finds and checks them, and a person verifies them every week. Each event shows whether it has been verified yet. It's built for people learning AI who don't have a technical background and would rather talk with people than sit through a sales pitch.

What this is

A checked table, not just a feed

Each row is one event, with the date, place, price and registration link taken from the organiser's own page. Anything not published says "TBC" instead of a guess.

Who it's for

Professionals in Switzerland who want to learn AI and think critically about it, whatever their background. It started for members of European Women in AI and is open to everyone.

Who's behind it

Built and reviewed by Wendy Look, Vice President of European Women in AI. The rules, code and change history are public on GitHub.

Upcoming events

New events appear as soon as the scan finds them, so you hear about them early. Verified means a person has checked the event. Not verified means only the scan has checked it so far; these are reviewed weekly. Click a row to see its details and registration link, and filter by type, audience and topic.

How to read the table

Connect

People talk to each other and no technical background is assumed: meetups, panels followed by an apéro, breakfasts, community nights.

Learn

Content first: conferences, talks, workshops and hackathons. For hackathons, Why says whether non-developers are welcome.

For

Who the organiser says it's for, such as Everyone, Business leaders, Founders, Product & UX or Developers. "Everyone" is only used when the organiser says so.

Domain

What the event is about, such as AI Agents, AI at Work & Productivity, or AI Governance, Ethics & Law. Up to three per event.

Approx Size

Small (under 40), Medium (40–150) or Large (150+). It tells you whether a real conversation is likely. If the organiser doesn't state a size, it's an estimate.

Status

"Verified" means a person has checked the event. "Not verified" means only the scan has checked it so far. Double-check those on the organiser's page.

Registration

"Open" means the link opened this event's registration page on the Last checked date. "Closed" means it was full or closed at that check.

How it works

The setup is deliberately simple: Airtable, a Claude scheduled task and a written rulebook. The human step is part of the design.

Assistant · Mon & Fri

Scan

Checks Swiss event calendars, Luma, Meetup groups and organiser sites against the rulebook.

Assistant

Verify

Opens each event's own page and records the facts with "TBC" for anything missing. It also re-checks links for events coming up soon.

Person

Review

Every new row arrives as "Not verified". Wendy reviews them weekly and marks each one Verified or Rejected. The assistant can't verify anything.

Public

Publish

New rows show up right away, labelled. Rejected rows are hidden.

An event is added only if:

  • it takes place in person in Switzerland, or online (trial since November 2026) when it starts between 08:00 and 21:00 Zurich time, is interactive, and isn't a product pitch;
  • AI is the main theme, not one panel inside a broader conference;
  • it's run in English (bilingual counts);
  • it isn't a pitch night, investor event, job fair, trade show or student-only event;
  • there's a page where you can register for this specific event. Events listed only on calendars or aggregator sites are left out.

Vendor-run events stay in when they're hands-on (workshops, labs), and the company is named. The full rules are in the rulebook.

Always check the organiser's page before you go. Events change, and our last check may be a few days old.

Want one for your country?

Everything that runs this list is open: the rulebook the assistant follows, the source list and the table design.

  1. Copy the table. Create an Airtable base with the fields listed in the rulebook. Decide what the public sees: only verified rows, or all rows with their status shown. This table shows everything except rejected rows.
  2. Adapt the rulebook. In skill/ewai-event-scan/SKILL.md, change the country, cities, sources and language rule to fit your community.
  3. Run it on a schedule. Add the rulebook as a skill in Claude and set up a scheduled task that runs it.
  4. Plan time to review. The assistant only suggests events. A person decides, and the scan finds more than you'd expect.
